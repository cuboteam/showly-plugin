# English editing

Adapted from Humanizer; see [upstream.md](upstream.md).

Look for sentences that announce importance instead of saying something, repeated
conclusions, mechanical rhythm and inflated claims. Diagnose them in context;
ordinary punctuation and common words are not evidence of machine authorship.

- Cut staged openings such as “Let's dive into” when they merely delay the point.
- Remove a “not X, but Y” contrast only when X is an invented foil. Keep meaningful
  distinctions and corrections the reader actually needs.
- Replace vague praise with the concrete information already in the source. If
  there is none, do not invent measurements, anecdotes or authority.
- Keep separate facts in lists even when there happen to be three. Merge repetition
  only when it repeats meaning, not merely sentence shape.
- Use direct verbs when they improve clarity. Preserve domain-specific vocabulary,
  genuine uncertainty, humor and deliberate asides.
- Remove chatbot greetings or offers left in standalone copy, while preserving
  appropriate salutations and sign-offs in actual correspondence.
- Use headings and emphasis for navigation, not decoration. Preserve document
  anchors and the writer's chosen conventions within the requested edit scope.

Example (fictional teaching copy):

Before: “Let's dive into a groundbreaking update. The export can contain up to
500 rows and is available only to administrators during the beta.”

After: “During the beta, administrators can export up to 500 rows.”

Keep: “The request was accepted, but the export has not finished.” Both halves
carry distinct status information; shortening this to “The export succeeded” is
an error.

Match a supplied voice sample's rhythm and punctuation without copying its facts.
A formal report should not acquire jokes, first-person reactions or invented
personal experience merely to sound human.

## Warmth without performed empathy

For an established delay, “We deeply understand how incredibly frustrating this
must be” can become “Sorry for the wait.” Keep the acknowledgment alongside the
status and next step; do not assert how the reader feels. Preserve a natural
“Thanks for bearing with us” when it fits the supplied voice. Do not scatter
thanks, apologies or exclamation marks through neutral technical prose.

A welcome or an invitation can be useful even when it adds no product fact.
Judge it by the relationship and genre, not by word count alone.
